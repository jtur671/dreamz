What you can tell a client

Three claims we can defend.

Every clinician-facing statement about Dreamz traces back to one of these three.

Private by design.

Dream content is encrypted at rest. We never train models on user content. The client owns their journal on their device — we do not offer a therapist dashboard, a shared record, or any cross-user analytics. What the client chooses to bring to session is the only thing you see.

Structured between-session homework.

Voice-to-text capture in under thirty seconds on waking. Tagged entries, recurring-symbol tracking, a searchable timeline, and a 5,700-entry reference library. Clients arrive with a readable log instead of foggy recall — and the friction is low enough that the homework actually gets done.

Recommend it without recommending a purchase.

The free tier — unlimited journaling, voice capture, full reference library, and one AI-assisted reflection prompt per day — is sufficient for weekly between-session work. Premium is optional ($5.99/month or $49.99/year). You can recommend Dreamz without effectively recommending a subscription.

Scope

What Dreamz is, and isn't.

We'd rather tell you where we don't fit than sell you on a version of the product we didn't build.

Designed for

  • Between-session dream journaling as a structured client practice
  • Depth-psychology and Jungian modalities where symbolic material is already a frame
  • Trauma-informed, EMDR, and somatic practices where nightmare and fragmented imagery matter
  • Psychedelic-integration work where dream activity spikes between sessions
  • Clinicians who want their client to journal consistently without adding clinical surface area

Not designed for

  • Clinical documentation, medical records, or any HIPAA-covered workflow
  • Symptom assessment, diagnosis, or clinical decision-making
  • In-session therapeutic intervention
  • Minors (App Store rated 17+; review with client before assigning to an adolescent, if at all)
  • Clients in acute crisis — the app shows crisis resources but is not a crisis-response system

In practice

How clinicians use Dreamz.

Four of the patterns we've seen most often during early clinician feedback.

Between-session homework

A Jungian analyst assigns dream journaling as ongoing practice. The client captures dreams the moment they wake and arrives at session with a tagged timeline and a symbol-pattern view — not foggy morning-after recall.

Intake and pattern-finding

A trauma-informed therapist begins work with a new client by asking them to journal dreams for four weeks. The recurring-symbol view surfaces themes — water, falling, locked doors — that become entry points for the first session's real work.

Nightmare and processing work

An EMDR clinician uses Dreamz as a structured way for a client to capture nightmare content between reprocessing sessions. The app frames distressing content with grounding prompts rather than symbolic interpretation; the clinician does the interpretive work in session.

Integration companion

A ketamine-assisted psychotherapy provider recommends Dreamz for the two-week integration window post-dose, when dream activity intensifies and structured capture becomes both easier and more valuable.

Questions we've heard

Clinician FAQ.

Direct answers to the questions we've heard most from early clinician conversations. Full FAQ available on request.

Is Dreamz HIPAA-compliant?

No. Dreamz is a consumer journaling app, not a protected-health-information system. The client owns their journal on a personal device; the therapist never receives client data through Dreamz. There is no clinician dashboard, no shared record, and no Business Associate relationship between Dreamz and a therapist who recommends the app.

A client choosing to show dream entries to their therapist during a session is the same as a client choosing to share a paper journal — the act of sharing is under the client's control, not the app's. That keeps us cleanly outside the boundary where HIPAA applies.

If your practice requires a HIPAA-covered journaling surface, Dreamz is not the right fit and we'll say so plainly.

The main site talks about "mystical readings" and a "grimoire." Is this a serious tool?

Fair question. The consumer brand voice is deliberate: it signals to a general wellness audience (tarot, astrology, journaling readers) that this is designed for reflection, not diagnosis. That voice exists on the public consumer page because it moves the product in a crowded consumer market.

The product itself is a structured dream journal with optional AI-generated reflection prompts. If you open the app you will see: a timeline of entries, tags, voice-to-text capture, a searchable archive, and a 5,700-entry symbol reference. The AI readings are framed as reflection prompts using explicitly hedged language ("may suggest", "often reflects") — never diagnostic, never predictive.

We recommend clinicians evaluate the product UI before forming an opinion from the consumer marketing alone.

What's in the AI readings — are they diagnostic?

No. A reading is a generated reflection prompt built from the symbols a client described. It names the symbols present, offers one possible interpretive framing, and suggests a journaling or grounding practice. Every interpretive sentence is hedged ("may reflect", "one reading of this is").

Our guardrails on the reading prompt explicitly forbid naming DSM conditions, suggesting diagnoses, recommending medications, or claiming interpretive accuracy. When a dream entry contains keywords consistent with acute distress, the reading is replaced with a grounding prompt and a link to 988 (US) and international crisis resources.

How do I recommend this without endorsing a paid product?

The free tier is designed for exactly this use case. Unlimited journaling, voice capture, full 5,700-symbol reference, and one AI reading per day. One reading per day is more than enough for a weekly between-session homework assignment. Most clients never need to upgrade.

When you recommend Dreamz, you are recommending a free app; the upgrade is the client's independent decision. Dreamz does not pay clinicians, affiliates, or referral partners.

What happens if a client journals a nightmare or discloses trauma?

The product treats distressing content gently. Self-harm and crisis keywords trigger a grounding prompt and crisis-line links (988 in the US; country-appropriate lines where available) in place of a standard reading.

Nightmare and trauma content produces a reading that names themes (fear, fragmentation, loss) but avoids pathologizing language and avoids interpreting trauma content back at the client. The reading suggests grounding practices — breath, body awareness, reaching out to a trusted person or therapist — rather than symbolic interpretation.

Dreamz is a journaling companion, not a crisis-response system. The safety guardrails are meaningful but not infallible. A visible crisis-resources link appears on every screen where reading output is shown.

What about data retention?

Dream content is encrypted at rest on device and in our backend. We do not train models on user content — this is in our privacy policy and enforced in our contract with the model provider. Deleting an account removes all dream content from our systems within 30 days. Account inactivity does not auto-delete content; the client remains in control.
